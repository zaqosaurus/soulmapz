# What the world holds that the survey does not

*2026-11-17. Companion to [STRANGER_REVIEW.md](STRANGER_REVIEW.md). That file looked at the
page; this one looks at the world. Written from memory with no fetches: every source, count and
licence below is a lead to verify at the acquisition gate, not a checked fact.*

## The diagnosis

Almost every layer in the survey has one of two authors: **a government counting bodies, or a
company siting buildings.** That is why the map is solemn and why it follows population. It is
one witness's testimony.

The soul leaves five other kinds of record, and the survey holds almost none of them.

## 1. Kindness is recorded. The roadmap looked in the wrong files.

NEXT.md says nothing counts the stranger helped, and asks for magic. It exists.

| Layer | What it is | Source lead |
|---|---|---|
| **The Wallet Came Back** | 17,303 "lost" wallets handed in at 355 cities in 40 countries; the share returned, city by city | Cohn et al., *Science* 2019; replication data published |
| **How Long a Stranger Waits** | Crowd-logged hitchhiking spots worldwide, each with a wait time: minutes until someone stops | Hitchwiki / Hitchmap dumps |
| **Who Ran Toward** | 10,000+ Carnegie Medals since 1904, each to a civilian who risked their life for a stranger, each with a place | Carnegie Hero Fund |
| **Neighbours Who Answer the Alarm** | Share of fire departments staffed by volunteers, by county | USFA National Fire Department Registry |
| **Gifts of the Body** | Registered organ donors as a share of adults, by state; living donations to strangers | Donate Life / OPTN |
| **A Card for No One You Know** | Postcards mailed to strangers per head, by country | Postcrossing statistics |
| **Laughed Yesterday / Helped a Stranger** | Share of people who say so, by country | World Happiness Report tables (Gallup) |

## 2. The soul reporting itself

Records nobody was required to make. Where people volunteered attention.

| Layer | What it is | Source lead |
|---|---|---|
| **Did You Feel It?** | Millions of citizen reports of felt earthquakes. A federal dataset whose name is this project's question | USGS DYFI, public domain |
| **Who Answered** | 2020 Census self-response rate by county: who replies when the country asks *are you there?* A survey's layer about answering surveys | census.gov (already reachable) |
| **Kneeling to Look** | Nature observations logged per resident: people photographing a beetle and telling the world | iNaturalist via GBIF |
| **Calling Anyone** | Amateur radio licences per head by ZIP: people who want to talk to strangers far away | FCC ULS, public domain |
| **The Rain Counters** | Volunteers who measure rainfall every morning, some for fifty years | CoCoRaHS, NWS Cooperative Observers |
| **The Reaching** | 988 calls answered by state and month | SAMHSA |
| **A Census of Play** | Every public pinball machine, kept current by players; every surviving wooden carousel and drive-in | Pinball Map API; National Carousel Association |

## 3. Names — the soul naming

The survey already holds the federal names file. It has been mined once.

| Layer | What it is | Source lead |
|---|---|---|
| **Homesick** | An arc from every New Somewhere to its Somewhere. Paris, Texas to Paris. Longing drawn as flows, beside The Uprooted | Wikidata `named after` (P138) |
| **What We Un-named** | The ~650 places renamed in 2022 to remove a slur, with old name, new name and coordinates. Moral revision, geocoded | U.S. Board on Geographic Names |
| **Hope, Paradise, Last Chance, Disappointment** | The emotional toponyms: Lonesome, Starvation, Nameless, Defeated Creek | GNIS (already in hand) |
| **What We Wish on Our Children** | The baby name each state over-uses most against the nation, by year since 1910. Legend, Messiah, Nevaeh | SSA, public domain |
| **Who Gets a Statue** | ~50,000 monuments audited. Mermaids outnumber congresswomen | Monument Lab National Monument Audit |
| **Who the Streets Remember** | Streets named for men versus women, city by city | OSM + Wikidata (EqualStreetNames method) |
| **Chosen Kin** | Sister-city pairs as arcs: towns that picked a friend across the world | Wikidata `twinned administrative body` (P190) |

## 4. Negative space — what is left, lost, or not there

After the population finding, this is the category that most changes the map.

| Layer | What it is | Source lead |
|---|---|---|
| **Nobody** | Census blocks with zero residents. Nearly half of all blocks in 2010. The photographic negative of every other layer | census.gov (reachable) |
| **Places That Stopped** | Every populated place the names file marks *(historical)*: towns that had a name and no longer exist | GNIS (already in hand) |
| **No One Came** | Counts by county of the unclaimed dead: identified, and nobody collected them | NamUs Unclaimed Persons (counts only, per the naming rule) |
| **The Holes We Left** | 117,000+ documented orphaned oil and gas wells | USGS, public domain |
| **More Graves Than Neighbours** | Cemeteries per living resident | GNIS (already in hand) |
| **What the Sea Kept** | Charted wrecks | NOAA AWOIS / ENC, public domain |
| **The Last Quiet** | Modelled natural sound levels, coast to coast. The partner to The Last Dark | National Park Service sound map |
| **The Last Payphones** | What remains | OSM `amenity=telephone` |
| **Where We Keep People** | Share of a county living in an institution: cell, barracks, dormitory, nursing home, convent. Some counties are mostly prison | Census group quarters (reachable) |

## The pairs

The survey's gesture is laying one sheet on another. The strange is in the pairs chosen on
purpose:

- What fell × what we said we saw
- Buried on purpose × buried by neglect
- Homesick arcs × refugee arcs: longing against flight
- Chosen kin × the world at war
- The last quiet × the last dark
- Did You Feel It × how hard the ground really shook: who speaks up
- The wallet came back × the company we keep
- Nobody × everything

## If only seven

Chosen for strangeness per credit: sources already in hand or already reachable come first.

1. **Nobody** — the negative of the map. Census.
2. **Places That Stopped** — GNIS, already downloaded.
3. **Homesick** — Wikidata, arcs engine exists.
4. **Who Answered** — Census; the survey looking at itself.
5. **The Wallet Came Back** — one published table; overturns the roadmap's premise.
6. **Did You Feel It?** — public domain, and the name alone earns the slot.
7. **What We Wish on Our Children** — SSA; the first layer here that will make someone laugh
   and then send it to their mother.

## Care

Several of these touch the dead and the wronged (unclaimed persons, lynchings, executions,
paddling). The existing rules cover them: counts for places, no private names, terms read
first. Three sources are crowd-made (hitchhiking, pinball, nature observations) and measure
who contributes as much as what exists; the plate must say so. The wallet study covers 355
cities and says nothing about anywhere else.
